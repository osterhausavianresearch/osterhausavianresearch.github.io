Methods

Different views of the same migration

I combine observations from the air and the ground to connect behavior, movement, and conservation. Each method contributes a different view of when, where, and how birds migrate.

Research Toolkit

Field observations meet remote sensing

Weather and research radar

Radar reveals the timing, intensity, direction, and altitude of movement through the atmosphere. I use weather surveillance and research radar to examine migration across scales and evaluate how radar observations compare with other monitoring approaches.

Nocturnal flight calls

Acoustic recorders capture the calls of birds migrating at night, adding species-level information to studies of passage and behavior. My work includes spatial arrays, automated acoustic processing, and integration of data from other sources.

Field experiments

Experiments manipulating light and acoustic stimuli help isolate the mechanisms behind behavioral responses. These studies connect observations of altered migration with questions about lighting characteristics, communication, and opportunities for mitigation.

Field surveys and mortality monitoring

Field observations ground remote sensing in conditions birds encounter. Work on carcass persistence and detection helps interpret mortality surveys, where scavenging and differences in detectability can affect the evidence available to researchers.

Large ecological datasets

Long-term banding records and community-science products make it possible to study migration across years and regions. I use these resources to examine demographic variation, migration timing and speed, and species-level differences in conservation coverage.

Conservation evaluation

I bring monitoring and ecological data together to evaluate the reach of conservation interventions. Comparing migration forecasts with species-level passage information can reveal where alerts work well and where their timing may leave gaps.

Integrating Evidence

What can we learn by combining methods?

No single dataset captures every part of migration. Comparing independent observations helps reveal what each method measures and where its limits matter.

Radar + acoustics

Connect broad patterns of aerial movement with species-level calling activity while evaluating differences between the two signals.

Banding + migration monitoring

Place age-specific timing and movement strategies in the context of seasonal and continental migration patterns.

Forecasts + species information

Assess whether the nights targeted for conservation also encompass the passage of different species.

Future Directions

Link passage aloft with life on the ground

I aim to connect nocturnal recording networks with daytime observations of stopover communities, and eventually with individual tracking. Together, these approaches could show how movement through the air relates to habitat use, disturbance, and survival across landscapes.
