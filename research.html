Research

Avian migration in the Anthropocene

My research seeks to understand how migratory birds respond to human-altered environments and how new monitoring approaches can make conservation action faster, more targeted, and more effective.

Research themes

I work at the intersection of migration ecology, conservation science and practice, and aeroecology. Across projects, I combine field observations, radar, acoustic data, and applied quantitative approaches to understand movement through the lower atmosphere and ecology on the ground to guide conservation efforts.

  • Behavioral and conservation consequences of artificial light at night.
  • Migration timing, speed, demography, and community composition.
  • Radar and acoustic methods for estimating nocturnal migration activity.
  • Decision-support tools for agencies, cities, conservation groups, and communities.
