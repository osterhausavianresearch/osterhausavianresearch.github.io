Lab Vision

A future lab connecting migration ecology and conservation

My vision is to bring together field research, emerging technologies, and conservation partnerships while training the next generation of ecologists and conservation professionals.

Future Lab Mission

Migration science with a conservation purpose

I envision a collaborative lab that explains how migratory birds respond to environmental change and turns that knowledge into practical conservation strategies. Research would connect individual behavior, landscape-scale movement, and the decisions that affect migratory populations.

Responses to environmental change

How do artificial light, altered landscapes, and extreme weather affect migratory behavior and survival? I combine field experiments and monitoring to identify when these changes create risks for birds.

From individuals to migration patterns

How do differences among individuals and age groups shape migration across a continent? I connect migration timing, speed, and demographic variation with the patterns observed across species, seasons, and landscapes.

Migration science into conservation action

When and where can conservation make the greatest difference? My future lab would work with conservation partners to identify threats, evaluate interventions, and develop strategies that protect migratory birds and the habitats they depend on.

Research Directions

A connected view of migration

Build a long-term acoustic network

Establish a spatially extensive recording array to examine how disturbance and natural environmental variation shape nocturnal migration within and among years.

Connect migration and stopover

Pair nighttime flight-call monitoring with daytime acoustic observations to investigate how migration aloft relates to habitat use on the ground.

Extend monitoring across seasons

Develop a platform for studying breeding, migration, and overwintering communities, with questions about habitat structure, land use, and environmental variability.

Integrate individual tracking

As tracking technologies become more accessible, combine individual movement data with radar and acoustics to investigate behavioral variation, exposure to disturbance, and survival.

Student Mentorship

Room for different questions and career paths

I want students to develop independent questions while building the technical and interpersonal skills they need for their chosen careers.

Training would span field ecology, remote sensing, bioacoustics, statistics, conservation science, and science communication.

Undergraduate research

Field deployments, acoustic data processing, and focused independent analyses offer entry points into ecological research.

Graduate and postdoctoral research

Researchers could lead experiments, connect datasets across projects, and develop independent questions within the lab’s broader themes.

Shared learning

Projects would bring together people with different experiences and strengths, creating opportunities to learn both methods and collaborative research skills.

Lab Culture

Careful science and generous collaboration

Inclusive mentorship

Support different backgrounds, experiences, and goals, and make room for students to contribute their perspectives to the research process.

Transparent research

Value careful study design, clear documentation, and transparent analysis so that findings can be understood, evaluated, and built upon.

Partnerships with purpose

Work with ecologists, statisticians, conservation organizations, and land managers to develop useful questions and share results that inform decisions.

Working Together

Research shaped by conservation needs

Partnerships would help connect fundamental questions with habitat management, biodiversity monitoring, and the evaluation of conservation actions. I aim to build projects that advance ecological understanding while giving collaborators information they can use.
