Teaching & Mentoring

Learning ecology through inquiry and experience

I connect classroom learning with field experience to help students become knowledgeable, inquisitive scientists who communicate clearly and apply what they learn beyond the classroom.

Teaching Philosophy

Knowledge students can put to work

I want students to leave my courses with a strong foundation in biology and the confidence, curiosity, and practical skills to keep learning throughout their careers.

My classroom and field courses connect scientific theory with ecological and conservation applications. Students practice asking questions, evaluating evidence, and communicating what they know.

Collaborative inquiry and peer teaching are central to this approach. Student-led discussions give learners opportunities to explain ideas, consider different perspectives, and pursue the next question together.

In Practice

Avian Field Ecology

At New Mexico State University, I progressed from laboratory teaching assistant to co-instructor and then full instructor of record across three offerings of this undergraduate/graduate course. By the final offering, I had developed the full suite of course content and instructional materials.

60%

Foundational instruction

Avian ecology, evolution, anatomy, physiology, and conservation.

30%

Student-led discussion

Seminal and emerging research, explained and examined with peers.

10%

Collaborative research

Small-group projects that turn student questions into research experience.

Learning in the field

Most laboratory periods took place outdoors, where students practiced bird identification, sampling methods, and careful observation. Field experiences built confidence in making decisions and discussing avian diversity, habitats, and ecological roles.
